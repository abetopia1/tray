Three names, one cork board. Nvidia, the most valuable company in the world. Megaspeed, a chip buyer few had heard of. And a businesswoman named Alice Huang. Follow the string.

October 2022. Washington bars sales of the most advanced AI chips to China.

2023. A Chinese gaming company spins off its Singapore arm. It becomes Megaspeed. Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, is its first managing director.

Then come the orders. The New York Times reports Megaspeed bought more than 4.8 billion dollars of Nvidia chips in two years.

October 2025. The Times reports the Commerce Department is investigating whether Megaspeed helped Chinese firms get around the ban. Nvidia says it found no evidence. Megaspeed says it obeys the law.

October 29th, 2026. The Times reports that US intelligence had been watching Alice Huang as a suspected Chinese asset, and that she had grown close to Nvidia's chief, Jensen Huang. They are not related.

November 8th. Jensen Huang writes to staff, quote, I was unfaithful to my wife. He names no one. Nvidia's board opens an independent investigation, and says it has no reason to believe secrets were compromised.

The next day, CBS and NBC report a Justice Department criminal investigation into Megaspeed. President Trump says the FBI is looking into it, and that Huang had, quote, absolutely no idea.

Now the other side. China calls the spy claim a smear. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing. Alice Huang has made no public comment. And no one has been charged.

Next pin: November 18th. Lawmakers have asked Jensen Huang to brief them behind closed doors.
